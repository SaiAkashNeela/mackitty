Technical Transparency

Built for developers and Mac power users who hate mystery bloatware.

Many Mac cleaners are heavy, subscription-driven and vague about what they delete. MacKitty is a small native Swift app that shows you exactly what it touches. Here is what happens under the hood.

Reproducible Ephemeral Targets
  • › ~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData — Intermediate object files, symbol indexes, and module caches that Xcode recreates automatically upon compilation.
  • › ~/Library/Caches — Safari, Chrome, Firefox and other app caches that apps rebuild on demand. Apple's own caches (iCloud, CloudKit) and caches of running apps are always skipped.
  • › ~/.npm — Cached npm package tarballs that are re-downloaded when needed.
  • › ~/Library/Logs — Old app logs and crash/diagnostic reports.
Strict Inviolable Boundaries
  • ✕ ~/Documents, ~/Desktop, ~/Downloads — Zero personal documents, photos, or desktop files are ever targeted.
  • ✕ .git/ repositories — Uncommitted source code, branches, stashes, and project files are strictly excluded from all scan routines.
  • ✕ Keychain & Browser sessions — Passwords, saved logins, cookies, and tokens are never read or removed; only browser cache folders are cleaned.
  • ✕ System & Signed Binaries — Read-only Apple system partitions (macOS Sealed System Volume) remain completely untouched.
Terminal — zsh — dry-run verification

Safety Guarantee

Why you can click "Allow" without a single worry.

When MacKitty initializes a scan, macOS Transparency, Consent, and Control (TCC) prompts appear on your screen. Here is why those popups occur and why your data is 100% safe.

The macOS TCC Permission Protocol

Since macOS Sonoma, Apple enforces strict folder-level access policies. When MacKitty scans for Xcode intermediate artifacts or system cache bloat, the operating system asks you to confirm read permission for that specific folder.

No personal data or files are ever deleted. Every single purge is explicitly presented to you in the Triage review screen, allowing you to uncheck any folder, inspect it in Finder with a single click, or skip it entirely.
